# 02 — Audit the Channel

Evaluate your partner base against three questions: are they closing, can they deliver, and can they staff a practice as you grow.

Since we were moving to a partner-driven strategy, delivery capacity was going to come from the channel. In parallel to recruiting new partners, the thirty already on the books had to be measured against what the model would ask of them.

The audit ran across every team on the Americas roster: what they were doing well, whether the relationship still made strategic sense, what was in their pipeline or had already closed.

A partner base that isn't producing is partly a vendor problem, so the audit needed to ask where we were falling short too. A few recurring gaps emerged: business support, technical support, training, lead generation, a standardized co-selling process, and the general quality of the day-to-day relationship.

Partners producing no revenue and without qualified implementation resources were cut. Each was sitting on five to fifteen licenses, kept current and supported through the same SLAs a paying customer got, generating no leads while being unreliable for implementation projects. Margins on this roster had drifted partner to partner with no consistent logic behind any of it. Some were carrying expired certifications, or had lost the staff who'd earned them. There was no retraining path and no performance plan, because there was nothing to remediate. A partnership holds only when both sides agree on what success looks like. Your program has to maintain that standard as it evolves. The audit created a floor: one set of terms, applied to everyone evenly.

Eighteen alliances didn't hold that line, and there was no version of this rebuild that kept a partner who wouldn't commit.
