# 01 — Define Your Ideal Partner Profile


## Understand the market

The worldwide CRM software market grew from $26.3 billion in 2015 to roughly $30 billion in 2016. Salesforce led with an 18.1% share, followed by Oracle at 9.4% and SAP at 7.2%, with Microsoft and Pega among the other established vendors. Each had an established channel in the region, years of signed agreements, and referenceable customers behind them.

Looking to capture the Americas market, the company was shifting from vendor-offered professional services to a partner-driven deployment model, reallocating roughly 300 resources to R&D. The opportunity for partners was to establish themselves as the regional go-to for implementation projects and to bootstrap future partners' initial deployments.

The product offered a unique proposition to take to market. CRM functionality sat on top of a BPM core, with referenceable SDKs, open APIs, and the ability to template processes and integrations (without coding) for end users. The platform brought enterprise-grade capabilities to the mid-market at a price point that opened opportunities with SMB customers. In Latin America, a combination of enterprise capabilities, SMB pricing, and language localization were key differentiators.


## Partner profile

Partner qualification centered on five practical criteria, with flexibility where a firm's capabilities or delivery model warranted it:

**Scale:** 50 or more employees on the integrator track, with flexibility where vertical coverage or delivery capacity warranted it.

**CRM experience:** Already working with a CRM competitor: category knowledge and existing client relationships in the space.

**BPM experience:** Hands-on experience with business process optimization, automation, and BPM tools.

**Professional services:** A real implementation practice: CRM, systems integration, or technology strategy.

**Technical capacity:** At minimum two people with working knowledge of C#, .NET, and SQL Server.

A firm's size should always be evaluated against actual delivery capacity rather than headcount alone. One of our strongest partners maintained a small U.S. presence while supporting more than 100 delivery personnel offshore.


## Segmentation

Segmentation determined how accounts were approached and how the partner proposition was presented.

The target universe ranged from global systems integrators to national firms, regional firms, and boutique specialists.

By business model, targets fell into three groups: sales-led organizations, services-led organizations, and technology partners including ISVs and integration specialists.

Messaging varied by organization, incumbent vendor relationship, and region. Sales-led firms cared about revenue: license economics, margins, and business development opportunity. Services-led firms were interested in what the platform could do for their clients and the delivery model: the shift from vendor-led implementation to partner-owned projects, and the opportunity to become the regional go-to. Technology partners were predominantly inbound and were evaluated on marketplace fit.


## Qualification

Qualification calls followed a repeatable structure, with slight deviation as needed. Conversations between sales oriented or services oriented orgs naturally varied.

In essence, these conversations were structured to immediately understand:

**Profile:** core business, founding date, office footprint, and regions served.

**Domain expertise:** CRM and adjacent technology platforms in active delivery, existing vendor relationships, client count, key accounts, and how the company generated implementations/project work or other revenue generating engagements.

**Partnership goals:** business model selection, revenue expectations, readiness to train and certify an implementation team, enable their sales and marketing teams, and whether the firm would draw on existing headcount or hire.

The qualification instrument used on these calls is included in the `tools/` folder in sanitized and generalized form.

## Geography

The program covered the full Americas, Canada and the United States up through Alaska, south through Central America and into South America as far as Chile.

Geographic segmentation ran parallel to everything above, with partner selection starting at the most local level available for implementation engagements and expanded outward as needed.

A partner who can be in the room within a day moves things faster than one flying in from another market.

Partners were allowed to operate across any geography. Deal registration was used to protect pipeline from cross-pollination by the vendor or other partners. Regional exclusivity was requested but never formally granted.

In Latin America, enterprise capability at SMB pricing, delivered in the local language, meant partners could provide organizations with offerings their budgets had previously excluded.

## Where attention went

Not every partner got the same level of hands-on involvement.

Three conditions moved a partner or opportunity to direct attention: full profile fit, a live competitive evaluation, or a foothold in a region or vertical the channel did not yet cover.

Those conditions shaped how resources and attention were allocated. A partner or opportunity that met them received prospect-branded materials, a white-labeled demo environment, and more direct personal involvement.
